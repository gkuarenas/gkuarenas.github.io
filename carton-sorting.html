Carton Defect Sorting

A camera-and-conveyor prototype that detects and sorts defective packaging cartons in real time with a YOLO11n model.

Overview

Packaging lines need to catch defective cartons before they ship, and manual inspection does not scale on a fast line. This prototype pairs a conveyor belt with a camera and a trained detection model to classify each carton as it passes, then acts on that call by moving a servo and lighting an LED. The build covers the full stack: the physical rig, the model, and the control loop that connects them.

Problem & Approach

Problem: Packaging lines need to catch defective cartons — open, torn, or holed boxes — before shipping. Manual inspection does not scale on a fast line.

Approach: The prototype conveyor belt runs on a 12V DC motor, with an ESP32-CAM for video capture, an Arduino Uno for control, and a servo motor plus LED for sorting and signaling. 170 labeled images across four box conditions (sealed, open, torn, holed) were collected, split 70/20/10 for training, validation, and testing, then augmented with flips, rotation, brightness, and noise to 510 images. A YOLO11n model trained on this set classifies each box condition. During live inference on the ESP32-CAM stream, a defect call inside a defined detection zone triggers a serial command to the Arduino, which moves the servo and lights the LED.

Key Features

  • Full-stack build: camera, detection model, microcontroller, and actuator working as one loop
  • Real-time defect classification across four carton conditions from a live video stream
  • Live LED and servo feedback that sorts a flagged carton without a person in the loop

Results & Outcomes

  • The YOLO11n model reached 0.872 precision, 0.948 recall, mAP50 of 0.970, and mAP50-95 of 0.796 across the four classes
  • The system caught and sorted defective boxes correctly during testing, at about 6 seconds per box end to end. That figure is total cycle time, not model inference speed: a deliberate 5-second LED hold after each defect call accounts for most of it, with about 1 second of stream, detection, and serial-command lag on top
  • The open-box class was the hardest to classify, and the cardboard prototype frame caused occasional servo stalling
