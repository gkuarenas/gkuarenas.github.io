GripVT

A wearable, camera-based sensor that estimates hand grip force from how a silicone skin deforms on the forearm.

Overview

GripVT is a visuotactile sensing system: a soft elastomer skin with a 99-point marker grid, worn on the forearm, watched by a fixed camera that tracks marker displacement as the wearer grips. It's for researchers and clinicians in biomechanics, rehab, and sports science who need a cheap, non-invasive way to estimate grip force without rigid dynamometers or multi-channel EMG rigs. A paper on the device has been submitted to IEEE Sensors Journal.

Problem & Approach

Problem: Grip strength is a standard clinical measure, but no non-invasive method captures the forearm deformation that produces it. Dynamometers give only one force number, EMG needs careful setup and drifts with fatigue, pressure gloves restrict motion. Visuotactile sensing solves this for object contact, but had never been tested on human skin driven by muscle, not external touch.

Approach: Mold a soft marker-grid skin, mount it over the forearm, track marker displacement with a camera as the wearer grips. Feed that displacement into an ML model, calibrated per subject, to predict grip force, validated against a hand dynamometer.

Key Features

  • Kalman filter + Hungarian assignment tracking across a 99-marker grid
  • Custom soft elastomer sensing skin, screened for compliance and marker visibility
  • Per-subject calibration mapping skin deformation to grip force, no load cells or EMG

Results & Outcomes

  • Grip-force estimates accurate to within ~6% of max effort, matching benchmarks set by published optical force-sensing systems
  • Validated end-to-end on real participant sessions with synchronized ground-truth force data
